# Independent review: polar terrain acceptance design

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-polar-terrain-design.md` and fetched the cited official PDS3 `dsmap_polar.cat` at:

<https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/lro-l-lola-3-rdr-v1/lrolol_1xxx/catalog/dsmap_polar.cat>

The catalog identifies `LRO-L-LOLA-4-GDR-V1.0`, polar stereographic, spherical radius 1,737,400 m, exact `MAP_SCALE`, and square images. Its north/south formulas are independently stated there. Neither `pyproj` nor `rasterio` was installed in this Python environment, so no independent library projection control was executed. The catalog URL returned the actual equations used below. This review covers the reference, design and analytical coordinate controls only; it does not claim source-code review or product/raster validation.

## Critical projection contradiction

The official catalog gives the inverse (line/sample to geographic) as:

```text
X = (I - N/2 - 0.5) * MAP_SCALE
Y = (J - N/2 - 0.5) * MAP_SCALE * NS
R = hypot(X,Y)
LON = atan2(X,Y)
LAT = sign-dependent polar stereographic inverse
NS = +1 north, -1 south
```

Here `I` is sample and `J` is line, in the catalog's one-based coordinate convention. Under this inverse, positive sample displacement means positive X and therefore +90° east longitude for either hemisphere; the south `NS=-1` changes line/Y direction, not sample/X direction. The catalog introduction agrees: longitude 90°E lies to the right in both hemispheres. My prior statement that the inverse makes south positive samples 90°W was incorrect and is retracted.

The actual contradiction is in the catalog's final forward equations: it defines `R = 2*1737400*TAN(...) * SIGN(LAT)` and then applies that signed R to both `X=R*SIN(LON)` and `Y=R*COS(LON)`. For southern latitude this reverses X as well as Y, which conflicts with the inverse and the introductory statement that +90°E is right. Treat this as an authoritative-catalog inconsistency requiring explicit reconciliation, not as evidence that the inverse or sample axis flips. Tests should implement the stated inverse independently and use it to derive expected projected coordinates; do not copy the contradictory final forward formula as the oracle. Record the chosen forward convention, cite the contradiction, and validate against product label/georeferencing and known decoded pixel controls before acceptance. File-array row order remains a separate matter from map line coordinates and must be established from product/decoder metadata.

For the requested site -89.9°, east longitude 0°, compute `R = 2*1,737,400*tan(0.1°/2) = 3,032.335812... m`; therefore `R/80 = 37.904197652...` pixels. The design candidate's actual product is `LDEM_80S_80M`; do not use an assumed N=1024 (that was only an illustrative size and is not the specified product). The product label's actual dimensions must be read at implementation/acceptance. With N=7600 and 80 m scale, the catalog inverse yields continuous one-based `I=3800.5`, `J=3800.5 - 37.904197652... = 3762.595802348...` for latitude -89.9°, longitude 0° (NS=-1). For +90° east, `I=3800.5 + 37.904197652... = 3838.404197652...`, `J=3800.5`; for -90° east, `I=3762.595802348...`, `J=3800.5`. In zero-based array coordinates these are sample/line `(3799.5, 3761.595802348...)`, `(3837.404197652..., 3799.5)`, and `(3761.595802348..., 3799.5)` respectively, before any decoder row-order mapping. These are analytical projection controls, not observations of actual product pixel values; confirm dimensions and scale from the label before treating them as product-specific.

For continuous coordinates, the inverse locates pixel centers at the catalog's half-offset coordinates; `NINT` in the final forward relation is for nearest-pixel assignment, not bilinear interpolation. Bilinear sampling must derive a continuous zero-based pixel-center coordinate consistent with the label and decoder transform, rather than applying `NINT` or treating integer array indices as projection centers. The design should explicitly state that convention and test it against known decoded pixel indices. A compressed JP2 source may not permit a simple direct byte-offset lookup of a logical pixel: verify samples through a trusted decoder, and use an independently constructed uncompressed control raster with known values to test the adapter's indexing/interpolation separately.

## Acceptance additions / clarifications

The design already usefully requires actual product metadata inspection, explicit coverage/NoData failure, bounded windows/work, exact-pole failure, registered-sky provenance and measured performance. Before implementation is accepted, make these checks concrete:

1. **Exact product and labels:** Verify the official product directory/catalog, actual file and label identity/version, bytes, dimensions, samples/lines, `MAP_SCALE`, hemisphere, map center, sample type/byte order, scaling and offset, special constants/NoData, valid latitude/longitude bounds, and published product checksum if available. Do not infer all constants from the filename or reuse the global 4ppd label assumptions. A locally calculated SHA is provenance, not publisher verification.
2. **Independent coordinate controls:** Add table-driven north and south controls using the catalog equations, including south `(-89.9°, 0°)`, `(-89.9°, +90°)`, `(-89.9°, -90°)` and asymmetric longitudes such as +30°/-30°. Test projected X/Y, continuous sample/line, inverse lat/lon, and actual array indices after separately verifying the file line order. Check wrap normalization and round trips at a tolerance justified by pixel scale. Test a known off-center pixel, not only center/symmetry cases. A center-only test cannot detect orientation/sign errors.
3. **Half-pixel and interpolation controls:** Use a tiny labeled synthetic raster with unique values per corner. Verify exact pixel-center reads, fractional bilinear values, corners and edges; show that the test fails if the `0.5` term is removed, axes swapped, or row order inverted. Verify the real product's decoded sample(s) against direct byte-offset reads and expected float/endian/scaling calculations. Preserve raw value, applied scale/offset and resulting radius/height units distinctly.
4. **Coverage semantics:** Distinguish nominal valid geographic product coverage from the square file footprint: the catalog explicitly says square corners extend beyond the stated latitude boundary. A point can be inside the raster rectangle but outside the product's valid latitude/coverage; do not accept such cells merely because they have indices. Define whether coverage is checked by inverse geographic domain, a documented valid-data mask, and/or product footprint. Check the entire requested 200 km radial path plus interpolation neighborhood, not only the site and endpoint; fail on any out-of-product or NoData sample rather than clamp, extrapolate, or silently skip an obstructing ray. Clarify whether a ray ending exactly on a coverage edge has enough neighbors for bilinear interpolation.
5. **NoData and numeric safety:** Test every declared special value before applying scale/offset; reject or mask NaN/infinity, malformed dimensions, truncated files, unsupported metadata, and nonfinite transformed coordinates. Propagate missing coverage as an explicit failure/warning according to policy, never convert it to zero height. Include negative and positive valid values and ensure radius remains physically positive.
6. **Bounded resources:** Give exact limits for download bytes, decoded/window bytes, maximum dimensions, window halo for bilinear interpolation, and maximum ray samples (`ceil(maxDistance/distanceStep) * bearings`) before allocations/loops. Test one-below, at-limit and one-above, including adversarial label dimensions and short/oversized payloads. State whether the window is read by seek/window or whether the full 115.52 MB product is temporarily decoded; the design's “no full Float32 global copy” alone does not establish peak memory. Measure peak process memory across fetch/decode/window/horizon/registration, not just the sampler. A sample-work cap must fail explicitly and must not silently coarsen requested resolution.
7. **Algorithm and result semantics:** Preserve the existing spherical horizon equation and reference sphere only if the selected product's radius-offset semantics truly match the 1,737,400 m sphere. Confirm frame and site-height registration use the same height datum. Validate azimuth orientation, great-circle destination and increasing distance; samples are not proof that narrow ridges are found. Report finite radial limit and edge coverage; no claim beyond 200 km or between sampled rays. At exact pole reject directional horizon requests unless an explicit azimuth convention is scientifically and mathematically documented.
8. **Integration/verification:** Exercise the actual decoder with the checked-in/downloaded product, then the known terrain producer -> existing registered-sky.py registration gate -> visibility path. Validate same site, frame, height and hashes end-to-end; include a deliberately mismatched frame/height/hash rejection. Run all tests without skipped acceptance tests. Compare 240 m/5° with finer radial and angular variants on this product and record horizon deltas and wall/peak-memory metrics. This is sensitivity evidence only, not truth/accuracy validation.

## Overall assessment

The acceptance direction is sound, particularly retaining the global adapter, requiring product-specific bounds/NoData handling, keeping the SPICE registration gate, and limiting claims. The projection sign/orientation ambiguity and pixel-center convention are acceptance-blocking until resolved against the label, raw pixels and independent controls. The catalog equations provide useful executable controls but cannot by themselves establish the actual selected raster's metadata or decoder row order. No product was downloaded or decoded in this review, no code/tests were changed, and no product acceptance or scientific accuracy is claimed.
